Dublin

A Viking port that became a Georgian capital

CountyCounty Dublin
RegionThe east coast
SectionCities

Dublin began as a Viking trading settlement at the ford of the hurdles on the River Liffey, and it has been the island's main port and capital ever since.

The eighteenth century left the city its shape: wide Georgian streets and squares, Trinity College, the Custom House and the Four Courts. The nineteenth left it the writers — this is the city of Joyce, Wilde, O'Casey and Beckett, and the pubs they wrote in still open at ten in the morning.

The essentials fit into a day: the Book of Kells and the Long Room at Trinity, the Ha'penny Bridge over the Liffey, Temple Bar for noise and Grafton Street for buskers, Kilmainham Gaol for the history that hurts, and the Guinness brewery for the view from the top.
