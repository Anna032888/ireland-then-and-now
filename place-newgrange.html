Newgrange

Older than the pyramids, and built for one morning a year

CountyCounty Meath
RegionBrú na Bóinne
SectionHeritage

Newgrange is a passage tomb of about 5,200 years, older than the Egyptian pyramids and older than Stonehenge. Its entrance is aligned so that, on the mornings around the winter solstice, the rising sun sends a shaft of light nineteen metres along the passage into the central chamber.

The mound is about eighty-five metres across and made of alternating layers of earth and stone, with a quartz facade that has been reconstructed — sometimes controversially — from what was found at its base.

The spiral carvings on the kerbstones and the roof-box above the entrance are among the finest megalithic art in Europe. Places in the chamber for the solstice are drawn by lottery, with tens of thousands of entries for a few places each year.
